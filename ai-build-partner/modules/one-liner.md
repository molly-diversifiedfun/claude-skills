<process>

**Step 0 — Check User Context first (Mode 1 behavior)**

Before the Opening, read `.unstuck/context.md`:
- Check **Section C** (project basics) if present
- If C.1 and C.2 are populated, DRAFT the one-liner from that context. Present it and ask the user to sharpen it. Skip Step 1.
- If context is empty, check if they just came from `/unstuck idea-bank` — if so, use the winning idea as the seed.
- If nothing exists, use the Opening below.

Don't ask what you can read. Draft what you can infer.

→ Next: **Opening** — set up the one-liner frame.

---

**Opening (output verbatim to the buyer):**

> "We're going to nail your idea down to one sentence. Not an elevator pitch. Not a tagline. Just: what you're building, who it's for, and what changes for them.
>
> The format: **'I'm building [X] for [Y] so they can [Z].'**
>
> If you can say it clearly, you can build it clearly. If you can't, we figure out why."

→ Next: **Step 1** — get the raw idea out.

---

**Step 1: What are you building?**

**What we're capturing:** The raw idea in their words — unpolished, unstructured.

"Tell me what you're building. Don't worry about making it sound good — just get it out."

**Example of a 5/5 raw answer:**
> "I want to make a course about writing better product specs. Like, junior PMs are out here writing these massive PRDs nobody reads, and I could teach them how to write one-pagers that actually get buy-in."

**Example of a vague answer to push back on:**
> "Something to help people with productivity."

Push back: "Help which people? With which part of productivity? Get specific — the specificity IS the product."

→ Next: **Step 2** — sharpen who it's for.

---

**Step 2: Who is it for?**

**What we're locking:** A person specific enough to picture. Not a demographic — a human.

"Who specifically? Not 'entrepreneurs' — give me a person. Job title, situation, what's frustrating them."

**Example of a 5/5 answer:**
> "Junior PMs at SaaS companies, 1-3 years in. They're stuck doing JIRA grooming and want to do real strategy work but nobody taught them how to write a strategy doc."

**Example of a vague answer to push back on:**
> "Anyone who wants to be more productive at work."

Push back: "That's 3 billion people. Who's the ONE person who'd text their friend about this?"

→ Next: **Step 3** — nail the outcome.

---

**Step 3: What changes for them?**

**What we're locking:** The before → after. Not what the product DOES — what changes in their LIFE.

"After they use this, what's different? Finish the sentence: 'Before, they ___. After, they ___.'"

**Example of a 5/5 answer:**
> "Before: they write 10-page PRDs that nobody reads and their EMs ignore. After: they write one-pagers that get buy-in in one meeting."

Push back if the outcome is vague ("they'll be more productive") or about the product ("they'll have a template"). The outcome is about THEM, not the product.

→ Next: **Step 4** — assemble and pressure-test.

---

**Step 4: Assemble the one-liner**

**What we're producing:** One sentence in the format: "I'm building [X] for [Y] so they can [Z]."

Draft the one-liner from their answers. Present it. Ask: "Is this right? Fix anything that's off."

**Example of a 5/5 one-liner:**
> "I'm building a spec-writing course for junior PMs so they can write one-pagers that get buy-in instead of PRDs that get ignored."

**Pressure tests (run internally, flag if any fail):**
- Can a stranger understand this in 5 seconds? (No jargon, no insider references)
- Is [Y] specific enough to picture one person? (Not "people" or "entrepreneurs")
- Is [Z] about their life, not about your product? (Not "so they can access my templates")
- Could you show this to 10 people TODAY and they'd know if it's for them? (The toy test)

If any pressure test fails, push back once with the specific fix. Don't wordsmith endlessly — good enough is good enough. Two rounds max.

→ Next: **Step 5** — save and route forward.

---

**Step 5: Save artifact + update context**

Save the one-liner to `.unstuck/one-liner-<YYYY-MM-DD>.md` using the Write tool. Include:
- The final one-liner
- The raw components ([X], [Y], [Z] broken out)
- Any pressure-test notes

Update `.unstuck/context.md`:
- **C.1:** Project name (extract from the one-liner)
- **C.2:** Problem space (the [Y]'s frustration)
- **C.3:** One-liner (the full sentence)

---
